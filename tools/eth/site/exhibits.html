02 · Carry math

ETH loops amplify staking.
Dollar carry adds a credit spread.

The debt currency changes the economics. ETH loops depend on the staking spread over ETH borrowing. Dollar carry retains ETH collateral and adds investment income minus dollar funding costs. One observed RLUSD loan lets us test that second spread.

01 · ETH debt

Loop the staking exposure

Stake ETH→Pledge the receipt→Borrow ETH↶

The borrowed ETH buys more staking exposure. Staking must earn more than the ETH loan costs. Token discounts, borrowing rates and the unwind can still hurt the investor.

Extra return ≈ (leverage − 1) × (staking yield − ETH funding cost)
02 · Dollar debt

Keep ETH, invest the dollars

ETH collateral→Borrow dollars→Dollar investment

The dollar investment pays income; the loan charges interest. ETH price risk remains in the collateral. Dollar assets must be redeemed and the debt repaid before the collateral can be released.

Extra return ≈ debt / equity × (investment yield − dollar funding cost)

Borrowing costs can rise, collateral can lose value, and exits can take longer than expected. Shared managers, tokens and borrowers also connect risks across protocols.

Debt and liquidation thresholds are fixed. For ETH debt, HF headroom refers to the LST/ETH conversion oracle; it is not automatically an ETH/USD drawdown or DEX depeg. For stablecoin debt, ETH/USD affects collateral value.

01Rate risk

A spread of tens of basis points is amplified by leverage. In the main Aave proxy model, +44 bp in borrow APR erases account return; +25 bp nearly eliminates the uplift over unlevered staking.

What to inspect

Net yield after debt cost, rates on each leg and utilization's response to product growth.

02Oracle and market

A CAPO conversion mark can differ from the DEX price. HF may hold while selling collateral becomes expensive. A 1% markdown of gross collateral can materially reduce loop equity.

What to inspect

Separate oracle, market-NAV and executable-unwind scenarios; check caps, rates and valuation units.

03Credit and self-credit

Dollar carry can depend on kBTC borrowers, PRIME credit and linked Morpho portfolios. A supply claim does not establish underlying recovery liquidity.

What to inspect

Who pays interest, who owns collateral, legal recovery rights and cash-flow independence.

04Exit waterfall

Selling a share, fulfilling a queue and unwinding the portfolio are three routes with different capacities. The borrow asset is needed before collateral can be released.

What to inspect

Cash, repayment routes, flash-loan rules, DEX depth, LST queues, maturity and bridge settlement.

05Who controls the assets and valuation

Check who can change prices, strategy permissions, fees and pauses. Liquid ETH has an operator fee-setting role alongside its owner timelock. Concrete's shared custody and internal shares also affect how investor capital is measured.

What to inspect

Authority, timelock, multisig, role history, Accountant age, liabilities and holder rights.

06Bridges and root backing

The rsETH incident and sunset networks show that cross-chain receipts depend on message verification and recovery procedures. Wrapped representations do not create new staking backing.

What to inspect

Lock/mint versus burn/mint, in-flight liabilities, issuer reserves, withdrawal eligibility and recovery deadlines.

06 · Playbook

If we build an ETH product,
the operating plan comes first.

Use measured financing constraints and investor outcomes to design the product. Separate a displayed rate from cash income, and book capital from executable withdrawals.

What it means for the product

  1. Secure funding before advertising a spread. Match the debt currency, capacity, caps and repricing terms to the destination and collateral.
  2. Design the exit as a sequence. Cash availability, destination redemption and lender repayment can have different time horizons.
  3. Make distribution accountable. Give exchanges, curators and other distributors a transparent return, fee and control record.

A better ETH product needs
a better result after exit.

The opportunity is to make extra income understandable, verifiable and worth the additional risk.

  1. Benchmark in ETH, over the same window, after all costs. Liquid ETH's two-year annualized return is 3.37%, versus 2.71% for stETH. A complex product must explain the additional income and how much survives redemption.
  2. Leverage cannot replace a durable spread. The 13.3× Aave example has a small rate buffer. A capacity model must include new borrowing's effect on the rate curve and available cash.
  3. Carry requires credit expertise. BTC collateral and home-equity finance are material ETH-yield dependencies. Curator quality and recovery matter alongside Ethereum contracts.
  4. A transparent exit can create product value. Independent NAV, time to cash, clear key control and reproducible stress scenarios provide information absent from an APY card.

    Glossary: APR, APY, PPS, HF, underlying and book NAV
    APR / APYAPR is an annual rate without compounding; APY includes compounding. Annualizing a short window does not promise a full-year result.
    PPS / sharePrice per share: accounting asset units per share. Convert the unit into ETH with the historical conversion rate.
    Book NAVPublished accounting valuation. Independent asset NAV requires assets minus liabilities, valuation and ownership evidence.
    HF / LT / LTVHF = collateral × liquidation threshold / debt. LTV = debt/collateral; permitted LTV and liquidation threshold differ.
    Loop leverage LGross collateral / account equity. r ≈ L × y − (L − 1) × b. Leverage amplifies the spread and cost/discount shocks.
    Carry / basisCollateral / USD carry retains ETH-long exposure. Spot / short basis largely neutralizes ETH/USD delta and produces a dollar return.
