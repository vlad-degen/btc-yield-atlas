02 · Carry math

What carry pays

An ETH loop borrows ETH to buy more staking exposure. Dollar carry keeps ETH as collateral and borrows dollars to invest elsewhere. Here we follow a measured dollar loan, separate base income from rewards, and test the spread after fees.

01 · ETH debt

Loop the staking exposure

Stake ETH→Pledge the receipt→Borrow ETH↶

The borrowed ETH buys more staking exposure. Staking must earn more than the ETH loan costs. Token discounts, borrowing rates and the unwind can still hurt the investor.

Extra return ≈ (leverage − 1) × (staking yield − ETH funding cost)
02 · Dollar debt

Keep ETH, invest the dollars

ETH collateral→Borrow dollars→Dollar investment

The dollar investment pays income; the loan charges interest. ETH price risk remains in the collateral. Dollar assets must be redeemed and the debt repaid before the collateral can be released.

Extra return ≈ debt / equity × (investment yield − dollar funding cost)

06 · Playbook

If we build an ETH product,
the operating plan comes first.

Use measured financing constraints and investor outcomes to design the product. Separate a displayed rate from cash income, and book capital from executable withdrawals.

What it means for the product

  1. Secure funding before advertising a spread. Match the debt currency, capacity, caps and repricing terms to the destination and collateral.
  2. Design the exit as a sequence. Cash availability, destination redemption and lender repayment can have different time horizons.
  3. Earn the right to add complexity. Compare net ETH outcomes with a staking receipt on identical dates. Explain the incremental income, leverage, credit exposure and exit cost before choosing a distributor.
