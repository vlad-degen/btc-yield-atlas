2 · How carry works

The trade in four steps

One loan against your Bitcoin, no re-borrowing. Each step has its own way to fail.

STEP 1
You hand over BTC
Today it becomes a token on another blockchain (kBTC, cbBTC, WBTC) and goes into a vault. You get vault shares; the growth of their BTC price, after fees, is your real yield. On our route the BTC stays on Bitcoin in a Babylon vault, and the lending market sees a record of it that cannot be moved.
Can fail: whoever controls the token. One Kraken key controls kBTC. On our route: the vault protocol, which is still on testnet.
STEP 2
The vault borrows dollars
It posts the token as collateral in a lending market and borrows stablecoins worth about 60 to 65% of it.
Can fail: a BTC drop of 18 to 35% gets the collateral sold (with Babylon vaults, whole vaults at a time), or the market runs out of dollars and the rate jumps.
STEP 3
It parks the dollars
In a lending vault, a loan token or a trading strategy that pays more than the loan costs. The gap is the profit.
Can fail: the parked asset loses value, as at Stream Finance in November 2025 and Resolv in March 2026.
STEP 4
It buys BTC with the profit
Each share is then worth a little more BTC.
Watch: the fee. At Kraken the operators keep 25% of a yield of about 1.4% before fees.

Words we use
Carry
Earning the gap between the rate you borrow at and the rate you lend at.
Wrapper
A token on another blockchain that stands for BTC held by someone: kBTC (Kraken), cbBTC (Coinbase), WBTC (BitGo), LBTC (Lombard).
Trustless Bitcoin Vault
Babylon's way to use BTC as collateral without a wrapper. The BTC stays on Bitcoin in its own output, and every way it can leave is signed in advance by you and the operators. Ethereum sees a record of it (vaultBTC) that cannot be transferred.
Aave v4 hub and spoke
The hub holds the money lenders supply; spokes are markets that borrow from it under their own rules. Babylon proposes a spoke that takes only vault BTC as collateral.
Vault Provider
The operator you pick to set up and redeem your vault. It cannot move the BTC outside the pre-signed paths and takes a commission when the BTC comes out.
LTV
Loan-to-value: the loan divided by the collateral. 64% means $64 borrowed per $100 of BTC.
Liquidation threshold
The LTV at which the market sells your collateral to repay the loan: 86% in Kraken's markets.
Utilization
How much of a lending pool is lent out. Above 90% the borrow rate jumps; at 100% it is four times normal.
Curator
The firm that decides where a vault's money goes and sets its risk limits: Sentora for Kraken, Gauntlet for Bitget.
Share price
How much BTC one vault share is worth.
Rewards, incentives
Extra payments from a token issuer or a blockchain on top of normal interest. They can stop at any time.
Timelock
A delay before an admin change takes effect, so users can leave first. It protects you only if it is longer than a withdrawal takes.
Multisig
An account that needs several keys to act. "1-of-1" means one key is enough.
EOA
A plain wallet run by one private key.
Health factor
The liquidation threshold divided by the current LTV. Below 1.0 the loan can be liquidated.
NAV
Net asset value: what the vault's holdings are worth, per share or in total.
Same block
Within one blockchain transaction: seconds, no waiting.

How it works

  1. A Kraken user taps Allocate, and the app's built-in wallet deposits the BTC as kBTC into the vault on Ink.
  2. The vault bridges the kBTC to Ethereum, posts it on Morpho and borrows RLUSD and PYUSD at about 3%, plus some USDT against WBTC on Aave and Morpho. Total debt: $298M.
  3. Two thirds of the dollars go back into Sentora's own lender vaults at about 6% with RLUSD and PYUSD rewards; one third into PRIME, a token backed by Figure home-equity loans, at about 6 to 7%.
  4. The profit is swapped back to kBTC; the share price rises at most 0.5% per update, at most every 6 hours. The 25% fee on the gain is split 80/15/5 across three addresses, with 15% to Sentora.

How it works

  1. You deposit BTC. The protocol borrows the same value in crvUSD from a credit line Curve's DAO minted for it, with no interest paid to Curve.
  2. Both go into a Curve BTC/crvUSD pool. The position is twice your deposit, half of it borrowed, which in theory cancels the pool's usual loss when BTC moves.
  3. Traders keep the leverage at 2x through arbitrage, so nobody is liquidated. The "interest" goes back into the pool to pay for that rebalancing.
  4. Unstaked shares earn the trading fees; staked shares give up the fees for YB tokens.

If we build one: native BTC on Aave v4

Our route: the BTC stays on Bitcoin in Babylon's Trustless Bitcoin Vaults and is borrowed against on Aave v4. The rules come from what the top five and the three closed products got right and wrong, and from the vault documentation.

The short version

  1. The wrapper risk is gone; the dollar risk is not. Native BTC removes the two weakest points we found: a wrapper on one key, and a price feed that cannot see the wrapper break. The yield still comes from the dollars: at Aave's USDC rate today (4.4%) the carry is about zero even with the rewards our own limits allow, and negative without them. It needs cheaper funding. Show rewards, funding deals and credit as separate lines.
  2. Treat the BTC as slow. It cannot be sold or topped up quickly: an exit takes about 3 days, a new vault about 2 hours (mostly waiting for 12 Bitcoin confirmations), and a liquidation takes whole vaults with up to a 10% bonus. So borrow less (50% against a 78% line), split every deposit into two vaults, and repay from the dollars within the hour.
  3. Line up the dollars before launch. Aave v4 had about $4M of USDC and USDT free to borrow on Ethereum on 23 September. A $100M product needs a supply programme on the hub with a named payer and an end date.
  4. Start where Babylon's BTC already is. About 80% of the BTC staked in Babylon is Kraken's custodial staking; Binance, OKX and Figment stake too. Those desks are the distribution. Self-custody users come through wallets.

How our route works

STEP 1
Lock BTC in two vaults
The depositor signs every way out in advance. Then the BTC moves into two Taproot outputs on Bitcoin: a front vault of about 60% and a protected one. Peg-in takes about 2 hours on testnet; if it stalls, the BTC comes back after about 14 days.
Can fail: the vault protocol: zero-knowledge proofs checked on Bitcoin, and the operators who challenge bad claims. The Security Council can block a payout but cannot move BTC.
STEP 2
Borrow on Aave v4
Activation records the vaults as collateral in the Babylon Core Lending Spoke. We borrow USDC or USDT from the Aave hub at 45 to 55% of the BTC's value. Liquidation starts at 78% on testnet.
Can fail: the hub runs short of dollars and the rate jumps, or Aave governance changes the terms or pauses the spoke.
STEP 3
Park the dollars
At least 70% where it can be pulled out and repaid in the same block, up to 25% in rewarded stablecoin vaults, at most 5% in credit. Never back into the hub we borrow from.
Can fail: the parked asset loses value or gets stuck, and with it our only fast way to repay.
STEP 4
Harvest, repay, exit
Profit repays the loan or is paid out as WBTC. To leave: repay, withdraw, then about 3 days of proof window before the BTC lands, minus the Vault Provider's commission.
Watch: fixed costs per vault (the peg-in fee, two Bitcoin transactions, the commission) eat small deposits.

What did not

  • Rewards without distribution. Bitget pays its own yield and stopped growing after three weeks. Yield Basis paid $11M in tokens and still shrank.
  • Points. Acre and Hyperbeat. Babylon's own stake peaked at about 61,000 BTC in December 2025 and is 32% lower now, as Lombard's LBTC and other staking tokens left.
  • Integrations promised, not delivered. Kraken grew without a single DeFi integration.
  • Quiet fee rises. Kraken's first week of outflows was the week its fee went up.

Three ways past 1%, and what we would not copy
Option A

Cheaper funding, disclosed

Borrowing at 1% instead of 4.4% takes the yield from about zero to about 1.4%. Routes: a supply programme on the Aave hub with a named payer and an end date, GHO at a rate Aave's DAO sets, or a borrowing reward. The catch: someone pays, and the product must work without it.

Option B

Collateral that earns on its own

BTC in a Babylon vault earns nothing by itself, and the docs do not say it can also be staked. A BABY reward on vault collateral would lift the yield, but it is a subsidy in our own token: publish it, give it an end date and price the product without it. Maple's partner-token subsidy ended in a court fight.

Option C

Win on transparency

Every vault is a Bitcoin output and every loan sits on Aave, so all of it can be shown live: positions, rewards and who pays them, hub utilization, the repayment ladder. It costs little and nobody does it.

What we would not copy. Anything the design and limits rule out, and book value that hides what an exit would pay.

Five questions to ask any BTC vault

What has the share price done? Not the APY on the page.
Which stablecoin does it borrow, and what is the yield without rewards? If it borrows RLUSD or PYUSD, probably close to zero.
Where exactly do the dollars go? A lender vault, a loan token, a trade. "DeFi strategies" is not an answer.
How much of the loan can it repay in the same block, and how full are the borrow caps?
Who holds the keys, and is the timelock longer than the exit?

Method, what we could not verify, and what changed

Method. On-chain reads of Ethereum, Ink, Base, Morph, Optimism, Monad, Rootstock, Core, Bitcoin, HyperEVM, Stacks and Mezo at the snapshot (20 September 2026, 12:00 UTC; ether.fi at 14:00 UTC) and again on 21 September; Blockscout for verified contracts, balances and accounting events; Morpho, Merkl and Upshift APIs; DefiLlama for protocols, yields and history; Binance daily prices; project documents, legal pages and SEC filings. The playbook also uses the Trustless Bitcoin Vault documentation and Aave's governance forum (read 23 September) and DefiLlama's Aave v4 and v3 markets on 23 September. Pools added after the 23 September check use each pool's own DefiLlama TVL series: the 20 September point for the snapshot and the last day of each month for the history.

What to count. Five switches, at the top of the page, above the market map and in the header menu, take a group out of every market number: the map, the shares, the two-year history, the text and the product list. Two more, off by default, add money markets and CDP collateral. They add up in any mix. A link that names any switch carries the whole choice (?babylon=off&mm=on; a switch it leaves out stays at its default); otherwise this browser's last choice applies.

The Babylon staking switch. Switched off, it takes out all BTC staked in Babylon: the direct stakers' row (37,648 BTC) and the Babylon stake inside four staking tokens (3,681 BTC), in the snapshot and in every month of the history. Each product's Babylon stake comes out of the category it sits in that month: LBTC's from options since August 2026, B² Buzz Farming's from farming, using the monthly split by finality provider in data/marketmap_overlap.md. In the link: ?babylon=off.

The lending switch. Switched off, it takes out the 15 products whose BTC is lent out: credit funds (Xapo Byzantine, Two Prime, Solv RWA), uncollateralized loans (Accountable, Wildcat, Native), Zest's sBTC lending market and vaults that only lend (Yearn, Harvest, Moonwell, Radpie, Superform, DeltaPrime, Extra Finance, Vesper). 3,153 BTC today; the list is data/lending_products.csv. Money markets have a switch of their own (below). In the link: ?lending=off.

The farming and pools switches. Farming and pools is split into three kinds, each with its own switch: points farming (BTC parked for points, airdrops or a protocol's own token; 4,334 BTC in 6 products today), strategy vaults (managed vaults that lend, loop or provide liquidity; 3,615 BTC in 22) and liquidity pools (DEX, perp and bridge pools over $1M and the vaults that stake their LP tokens; 2,554 BTC in 22). The fourth part, Zest's lending market and eight vaults that only lend (874 BTC), goes with the lending switch. The kind of each product and the BTC tokens it holds are in data/c6_groups.csv; the history uses the same split in every month. The line between points farming and strategy vaults follows the product's form, not its source of yield: many vaults also farmed points in 2024 and 2025. In the link: ?points=off, ?vaults=off, ?pools=off.

The money market and CDP switches. Off by default. Switched on, they add the BTC in lending markets (Aave, Morpho, Spark, Venus, Compound, JustLend and 58 more, plus eight small lending venues) and in stablecoin CDPs (27 protocols, 25 of them holding BTC today) that no product on the map already counts: 159,433 and 4,242 BTC on 20 September. From DefiLlama's protocol data (collateral plus unlent supply, in BTC units) we take out the staking tokens counted at their issuer (6,989 BTC, mostly LBTC), the positions of counted products measured on-chain (7,324 BTC, mostly the Kraken vault's 5,833 BTC on Morpho and Aave) and the lending products that are on the map already (Zest v2, Accountable, Wildcat, Native). Strategy vaults whose venues we could not trace may hold up to 3,720 BTC more of it. Some of these positions exist only at the snapshot, so earlier months overstate this BTC a little. It earns about 0.03% a year. About 37,900 BTC of it is the cbBTC behind Coinbase's BTC-backed loans on Morpho. Data: data/money_markets.csv; method: tools/marketmap/money_markets/mm_notes.md. In the link: ?mm=on, ?cdp=on.
