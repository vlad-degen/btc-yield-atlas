What it means for us

  1. Carry is a subsidy. 82% of Kraken's carry is stablecoin rewards paid through Sentora's wallets, and Bitget pays its own vault. Without rewards Kraken's vault would pay 0.1 to 0.6% a year, and Bitget's would lose money.
  2. Distribution, not yield, brings deposits. Kraken raised $527M at under 2% through a button in its app. The desks that own this flow already stake client BTC with Babylon.
  3. On Aave v4 the dollars are the constraint. At Aave's 4.4% USDC rate the carry is about zero, and Aave v4 had about $4M of stablecoins free to borrow on 23 September. A product needs a funding programme with a named payer.

Out along the top, back along the bottom. The loop: the dollars borrowed on Morpho go back into the Sentora vaults that lend on it. Boxes and steps link to the contracts and markets.

  2. The kBTC goes into the vault, a Veda BoringVault at the same address on Ink and Ethereum: 6,493 BTC from 37,572 users. Veda wrote the contracts; Sentora runs the strategy.

How the money moves

All on Ethereum. The borrowed crvUSD never leaves the pool, and the loan's interest is paid back into it.
BTC and pool tokenscrvUSDFees, rewards and profit
  1. You deposit WBTC, cbBTC or tBTC on Yield Basis and get shares of one of three markets: yb-WBTC, yb-cbBTC or yb-tBTC.
  2. Your BTC goes into a Curve BTC/crvUSD pool...
  3. ...with crvUSD of the same value, borrowed from a credit line Curve's DAO minted into Yield Basis (the factory holds the unused part). Curve earns no interest: the debt's 1.8% a year is paid back into the pool.
  4. Traders pay a 1.3% fee per swap: the only outside income. Arbitrageurs keep the position at 2x, so nobody is liquidated.
  5. The pool's gains are the fees minus the cost of re-pricing it to the market, 30 to 60% of the fees.
  6. 10 to 72% of each gain goes to YB lockers. Stakers give up the fees for YB tokens, about 5% a year in a token down 87%.
  7. You exit at the pool's value, not book: about 6% below book in the WBTC and tBTC markets on 21 September, up to 21% in February. Unstaked holders made −3.0% to +0.6% over 118 days.

How the money moves

The loan as of 20 September, the rest 21 September. After the bridge everything is on Morph, and the USDC goes round one Morpho market.
BTC and bgBTCUSDCRewards and profit
  1. You buy BGBTC on Bitget, a balance on its books. Bitget keeps 1,420 BTC in one address (120% of BGBTC, per a Chainlink reserve feed that reads Bitget's own list) and mints the token with no on-chain reserve check; one Bitget key controls the token, the bridge and the vault.
  2. One Bitget wallet bridges it to Morph and deposits 800 bgBTC into an Aera vault that Gauntlet runs.
  3. The vault posts 801.6 bgBTC on a Morpho market on Morph and borrows USDC: $40.6M (LTV 62%, liquidation at 77%).
  4. All the USDC goes into Gauntlet's USDC vault.
  5. The loop: that vault lends the USDC straight back to the same market. It is the market's only lender, and the BTC vault owns 82% of it.
  6. 1,386 outside wallets add $9.35M: almost the only real cash in the loop.
  7. Bitget's hot wallet pays $38.7k of USDC a week through a reward distributor; about 82% reaches the BTC vault, is swapped into bgBTC and added to collateral. Without it the loop loses money.
  8. The vault earns 2.34% a year; Bitget pays you 1.70% from its own books.
  9. You exit in 4 days, or at once for 0.1%. On-chain the BTC can leave Morph at only 65 bgBTC a day.

6 · Playbook

If we build one on Aave v4

What the top five imply for a carry product on Babylon vaults.

  1. Line up the dollars first. At Aave's 4.4% USDC rate the carry is about zero with parking rewards and negative without, and on 23 September Aave v4 had about $4M of USDC and USDT free to borrow on Ethereum. A $100M product needs a hub supply programme from a stablecoin issuer or Aave, with a named payer and an end date.
  2. Design for a slow exit. Borrow 50% against the 78% line, split each position over at least two vaults and repay from the dollars automatically: with a 3-day exit and whole-vault liquidations there is no time to top up.
  3. Go through the desks. Kraken's in-app vault raised $527M at under 2%, and Kraken is also the largest staker in Babylon. The desks that stake client BTC with Babylon are the distribution.

7 · Data

Method and sources

Figures as of 20 September 2026 unless marked.

Method

Counting. Each BTC is counted once, at the product the holder owns. On-chain reads of Ethereum, Ink, Base, Morph, Optimism, Monad, Rootstock, Core, Bitcoin, HyperEVM, Stacks and Mezo at 20 September 2026, 12:00 UTC; Morpho, Merkl, Upshift and Midas APIs; DefiLlama for protocols, pools and history; Binance prices; project documents and filings.

Left out. Wrappers that earn nothing on their own (WBTC, cbBTC, kBTC, bgBTC and others), bridges, yield-trading venues whose BTC is counted elsewhere (Pendle), dollar products partly backed by BTC (Ethena, Falcon) and pools of $1M or less.

Categories and switches. Each category has a switch that takes it out of every market number. Money markets and CDP collateral earn about 0% and are off by default: 160,306 BTC supplied to lending markets (Aave, Morpho, Spark, Zest and others, directly or through vaults that only lend) and 4,242 BTC posted for stablecoins, counting only BTC that no product on the map holds. Credit funds and uncollateralized lending pools that pay a yield are counted, as credit. Lending BTC itself pays about nothing: on 24 September the BTC pools paying more than 0.5% a year held $25M in all. A link carries the choice, for example ?staking=off&mm=on.

Money markets and CDPs. DefiLlama's collateral plus unlent supply, less staking tokens counted at their issuer and positions of counted products. About 37,900 BTC of it is the cbBTC behind Coinbase's BTC loans. Method: mm_notes.md.

Judgement calls. Avalon CeDeFi (1,283 BTC against a USDT loan) is not carry: it is a pool for four institutional accounts, its dollars went to Binance, and nothing has moved since November 2024. Midas mHyperBTC is carry: its strategy wallet posts cbBTC and borrows dollars. Lombard's BTCe is credit, not carry: LBTC posted as slashable cover for a market maker's loan. Yield Basis counts what depositors own, not the borrowed half of its pools. Veda's other vaults are counted net of Lombard's LBTCv (885 BTC) and of the LBTC held by ether.fi's eBTC vault (247 BTC at the snapshot, up to 3,000 BTC in early 2025), which DefiLlama values inside Veda as WBTC and which the Lombard Vaults and LBTC rows already hold; the history nets both month by month. DEX pools count only BTC not counted elsewhere: without the staking-token side of a pair (LBTC, eBTC, uniBTC, pumpBTC, stBTC), LP held by Stake DAO, Beefy or Troves, and the Kraken vault's own Uniswap position.

Not verified. Who funds the RLUSD and PYUSD rewards; who controls the unlabelled keys; fees at Coinbase's fund and Lombard Bitcoin Earn; Gauntlet's pay from Bitget. Aave v4 and vault terms in the playbook are testnet values or from the governance proposal.
