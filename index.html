Borrowing dollars against Bitcoin to earn a yield on Bitcoin: the vaults, the numbers, the catch

A growing family of products takes your BTC, posts it as collateral, borrows stablecoins against it and parks them somewhere that pays. Kraken runs the biggest one. We opened every vault of this kind on the blockchain, read the share prices to see what they really paid, and mapped the borrow markets they all depend on. Every number links to where it came from.

If you only read one box

  1. The trade pays 1 to 2% a year in BTC, not the 2.5 to 5% on the posters. Measured from share prices: Kraken 1.40% since launch and falling month by month, ether.fi Liquid BTC 1.95% over the last year, Lombard's LBTCv 0.98% over the last year.
  2. Most of that comes from someone else's marketing budget. In Kraken's vault about three quarters of the yield is Ripple and PayPal paying lenders to use RLUSD and PYUSD. On the organic rates alone the loan costs more than the parking pays.
  3. The plain version of the trade loses money. Borrowing USDC against BTC costs 4.6 to 4.8%; the safe places to put USDC pay 3.5 to 5%. Every vault that earns something is using a subsidized stablecoin, a credit token, or a token reward.
  4. The market is Coinbase, then everyone else. Of the $2.38B borrowed against BTC on Morpho, $1.43B is Coinbase's loan product on Base and $1.87B is in USDC. The subsidized RLUSD and PYUSD markets that make the vaults work hold $341M and are already 84 to 90% lent out.
  5. Every figure links to its source. Blue text goes to the block explorer, the lending market, the DefiLlama pool, or the document. Where a number is ours, we say so.

Words we use. LTV is the loan divided by the collateral. Liquidation threshold is the LTV at which the collateral gets sold. Utilization is how much of a lending pool is lent out; above about 90% the borrow rate jumps. Carry is earning the difference between two rates. Curator is the firm that decides where a vault's money goes. Share price is how much BTC one vault share is worth; its growth is the yield you actually got.

Part I

How the trade works, and why it is hard to make it pay

Four steps, each with its own way to fail. Then the arithmetic, which is where most of the trouble is.

STEP 1
You deposit BTC
It is wrapped into a token that works on the chain where the vault lives: kBTC, cbBTC, WBTC, LBTC. You get a share token back, and the share price is your yield.
What can go wrong: the wrapper's custodian or signers. Sky dropped WBTC as collateral in 2024 over a custody change.
STEP 2
The vault borrows dollars
It posts the wrapped BTC as collateral in a lending market and borrows a stablecoin, usually 60 to 65% of the collateral's value.
What can go wrong: BTC drops 25% and the position is liquidated; or the market runs out of dollars and the borrow rate jumps.
STEP 3
It parks the dollars
In a lending vault, a T-bill token, a credit token, or a trade. The spread between this rate and the borrow rate, times the LTV, is your yield.
What can go wrong: the parking asset loses its peg. That is what broke in October 2025, at Stream, and at Resolv.
STEP 4
It buys BTC with the profit
Surplus dollars are swapped back into BTC and added to the vault, so each share is worth slightly more BTC over time.
What can go wrong: the fee. At 25% of a 1.9% gross yield, the operator keeps a third of what the trade makes.

The problem: Bitcoin earns nothing, and the loan is not cheap

What lending BTC pays (left bars) is zero. What the borrowed dollars earn is 3.5 to 5% in the safe places, and more only with rewards. Borrowing USDC against BTC costs 4.6 to 4.8%. DefiLlama

The risk in this trade is almost never Bitcoin itself. It is the asset the dollars were parked in, the price oracle, the operator's key, and the loops between one curator's vaults and another's.

Part VI

What to do with this

If you are building one

Attack the cost of the loan

The gap between 3.25% (Kraken's blended borrow rate) and 1% (Mezo's MUSD) is worth about 1.4 points of gross yield at 64% LTV. It is the only lever that adds yield without adding risk. Routes: your own stablecoin, a subsidy deal with an issuer, or an institutional credit line at 1 to 2%.

Make the collateral itself pay

Hermetica gets 5.6% because its sBTC collateral earns 3.5% on its own before the carry. LBTC with the Bitwise covered call as collateral is the only route to 3 to 4% without incentives. The catch: LBTC borrow markets on Morpho are tiny, about $4M against USDC.

Take the empty middle

The market is split between 0.5 to 2% (safe and boring: Kraken, ether.fi) and 5 to 14% (opaque: Acre lost 1.24%, Maple is under an injunction). Publish the position addresses, the allocation by leg, and the share of yield that is incentives. Nobody does it, and it costs nothing.

What we would not do. Park the borrowed dollars in your own lender vault (the Sentora loop). Park them in an RWA token with fewer than 500 holders and no published redemption terms. Re-borrow against the parked dollars. Charge 25% on a yield below 2%. Park in anything that is not the same currency as the debt, which is exactly what failed in October 2025, at Stream and at Resolv.

If you are depositing into one

The realistic range

Measured from share prices, the vaults in this category have paid 1 to 2% a year in BTC, and the biggest one is drifting down toward 1%. Getting to 3 to 4% needs collateral that earns on its own or credit risk that should be disclosed. Anything above 5% in BTC is a basis trade, a loan to an institution, or token emissions.

Demand barely reacts to the rate. Kraken raised $521M on a product paying under 2%; Lombard Bitcoin Earn took 447 BTC in six months. Demand reacts to the brand, how easy it is to get in, and whether anyone has lost money.

Four questions to ask any BTC vault

Which stablecoin does it borrow, and at what rate? If the answer is a subsidized one (RLUSD, PYUSD), ask what the yield is without the subsidy.
Where exactly do the dollars go? A lender vault, a T-bill token, a credit token, a trade. Each fails differently. "DeFi strategies" is not an answer.
What has the share price done? Not the APY on the page. The accountant contract's rate history is public for every Veda vault; ask for the equivalent elsewhere.
How long does an exit take when the borrow market is 90% lent out? The stated 3 to 5 days assumes liquidity. Ask what happens without it.
